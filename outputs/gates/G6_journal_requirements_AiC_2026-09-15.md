## 2. Fit and risk assessment

### 2.1 What the project actually is, as of 2026-09-15

Read for this assessment: `protocol/AiC_详细执行方案_中文.md` (v1, 2026-09-15), `manuscript/Manuscript_AiC_WORKING_DRAFT.md` / `.tex`, `protocol/research_gates.json`, `README_先读我.md`, and the project's own outputs (`outputs/verification_report.json`, `outputs/gfs_knmi_calibration_pilot.json`, `outputs/closest_fulltext_audit_2026-09-15.md`, `outputs/dslib_construction_inventory.csv`).

**The project is a rigorously self-limited *research-design + implementation-verification* package.** Its carried evidence is:

- a state-dependent weather-window formulation (initiation threshold ≠ continuation threshold), three-valued window logic, and a non-anticipative information boundary;
- a deterministic time-indexed MILP kernel plus an **independently written** schedule verifier;
- 26,244 cross-implementation comparisons with zero differences on 6,561 constructed trajectories; **42 passing unit tests** (7 for the event executor);
- a constructed 4-task instance solved to a 13-slot makespan;
- two constructed counterexamples (favourable-hour count ≠ window count; equal hourly marginals ⇒ different joint window probability);
- a **pilot-level** empirical footprint: 120,600 KNMI station-hour records (3 stations, 2021–2025), **124** archived GFS six-hour forecast messages (January 2025 only, single lead, one station), a chronological 63/28/33 endpoint split, and one threshold that has **zero positive cases** so it cannot be calibrated;
- **no** executed main scheduling-value experiment; the DSLIB intake is still only 163 *candidate* metadata rows and no network has passed the rights/activity/resource review.

The project's own gate file says this plainly: `G2` OPEN, `G3` PARTIAL, `G5` NOT_EXECUTED, `submission_ready: false`, and *"PASS on the constructed kernel cannot close empirical or novelty gates."* The manuscript itself is labelled **"DEVELOPMENT MANUSCRIPT — NOT FOR SUBMISSION"**.

### 2.2 Does the design fit AiC's scope?

**Partly — the fit is real but currently one ingredient short.**

*For.* AiC's stated scope explicitly includes **decision support systems**, **process simulation models**, and **intelligent control systems** across the construction life cycle (row 1). A forecast-to-decision formulation for weather-sensitive operations is squarely "Information Technologies … Construction Technologies". Independent evidence that the journal accepts this material exists both historically (Ballesteros-Pérez et al. 2017, AiC 84:81–95, `10.1016/j.autcon.2017.08.022`) and in the current window: our Crossref sweep of ISSN 0926-5805 for 2023-01-01 → 2026-09-15 returned **2,140** records, of which the closest neighbours are risk-aware construction scheduling (`10.1016/j.autcon.2026.106957`), delay-risk prioritisation for weather-sensitive earthwork (`10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106588`), scheduling under uncertainty (`10.1016/j.autcon.2024.105428`), automated schedule generation (`10.1016/j.autcon.2024.105622`) and construction plan/control decision support (`10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106066`) — see `outputs/gates/G6_recent_AiC_similar_papers.csv` (all 10 DOIs independently re-verified by the auditor: 10/10 resolve, journal and year match).

*Against — and this is the crux.* Across those same 2,140 AiC records, **only 9 titles in 2023–2026 contain a weather/climate term at all, and none of them couples weather with project scheduling.** The papers AiC actually publishes in this neighbourhood are *applied and result-bearing*: a heat-risk digital twin, a wind-borne-debris predictor, a delay-risk prioritisation framework, a learning-based scheduler. Our manuscript currently delivers a **formulation plus verification**, with the decision-value experiment explicitly deferred. AiC is an *Automation*-flavoured venue: it expects the automated/decision artefact to be exercised on a construction problem with reported outcomes, not only proven internally consistent.

One caveat that must be stated whenever this gap argument is used: **Crossref holds no abstracts for AiC (0/2,140 records)**, so the sweep can only see titles. A closer AiC precedent might exist whose abstract mentions weather but whose title does not. Closing that residual risk needs a ScienceDirect/Scopus full-text search, which the 403 block prevented here.

### 2.3 Desk-reject risk verdict

**Verdict: HIGH desk-reject risk *as the manuscript stands today*; MODERATE and falling if the main experiment is executed first.** Two independent reasons:

1. **Result-bearing-content risk (primary).** The submission would be evaluated on constructed verification results, 42 unit tests, a 124-run single-lead single-station alignment pilot and a rejected one-class fit, while the manuscript's own Conclusions say no decision-value claim can yet be drawn. Editors at a 10+ IF automation journal routinely desk-reject a paper whose headline finding is "a research design and its evidence limits". Sections 6.3 and 6.4 are, by their own wording, *status reports* ("No project-level experiment … has yet been executed", "supplies a screening frame rather than 163 validated construction cases").
2. **Novelty-closure risk (secondary).** `G1` is still PARTIAL: final-version equivalence for Kerkhove & Vanhoucke (Omega 2017) and the full method of Zhou et al. (C&IE 2021) remain unread. The manuscript concedes that "continuous execution with asymmetric initiation and continuation is a different operation contract" must be argued on a declared common problem. An editor who knows the 2017 AiC weather-aware planning tool and the Omega offshore-scheduling line will ask what is new beyond the representation.

Aggravating factors specific to this journal: an ambitious single-blind review with at least two expert referees (row 34), the journal's own transfer service (editors actively re-route unsuitable papers), and the fact that AiC's community norm is a validated application.

### 2.5 (b) AiC-specific formatting/structure gaps in the current `.tex`

- **No Highlights, no graphical abstract, no CRediT, no competing-interest block, no `[dataset]` data references.** The `.tex` ends with a hand-written *"Declarations and reproducibility"* section that is not the journal's required declaration set.
- **The Elsevier LaTeX route is not used.** The build is a Pandoc-scaffolded `article` class with `secnumdepth` set to `-\maxdimen`, i.e. **section numbers are deliberately suppressed**, while the guide requires "clearly defined and numbered sections … Subsections should be numbered 1.1" (row: *Article structure*). The guide also recommends **`elsarticle.cls`** (row 23).
